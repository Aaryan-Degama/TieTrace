TieTrace
Check a storyHow it works

Every tie has a DOI. Every "no tie" has a scope.

TieTrace helps a journalist check how connected an expert is to the company in a story. It answers from a research graph built from OpenAlex and stored in FalkorDB, with evidence, not opinions.

1The problem

A story quotes a scientist. Was their work funded by the company the story is about, or did they list an affiliation with it? Reporters rarely have time to read a whole publication record. TieTrace reads it for them and shows each tie with the paper it rests on.

2The four steps

  1. Paste the story. Claude Code reads it and picks the research case for the company and topic.
  2. Names are resolved to OpenAlex author records. If a name matches more than one record, you pick; it never guesses.
  3. Ties are checked in the graph with read-only queries. The live evidence panel shows every query as it runs.
  4. The answer comes back per expert as "Expert #n", with DOIs for every tie and the scope for every "no tie found".

3What the tiers mean

TierMeaningHow to use it
A1Authored a small paper (25 authors or fewer) funded by the company, or listed a company affiliation on oneDirect evidence, with DOIs
A2Foundation grant, bottler affiliation, or the only evidence is a large multi-author paperDirect evidence, with DOIs and the reason
B2 or more recent small papers (since 2021) with a Tier A personNetwork context only, not validated
CWithin 2-3 such co-author hops of a Tier A personNetwork context only, not validated
NONENothing above within 3 hops"No tie found in the research record", always with its scope

A1 and A2 are direct evidence. B and C are network context only: they describe who wrote papers with whom, and our own tests (below) showed they are not a sign of unusual closeness.

4Why "no tie found" is never bare

A missing tie can simply mean missing data. So every "no tie found" states:

  • Hops: how far through co-authors it searched (up to 3).
  • Years: which years of collaboration count as recent (since 2021).
  • Funder coverage: how much of the data records funders at all, for the whole graph and for that expert's own works.

In the Coca-Cola graph only 56.3% of the 30,140 loaded topic works list a funder (96.6% list a funder or an affiliation, but affiliation says little about funding). In the Philip Morris graph it is 37.5% (7,482 of 19,964), so "no tie found" is weaker there. The topic part of the graph is a sample of the most-cited works per year, about 11.5% of the field.

5What we tested, honestly

Pass rules were fixed before the runs and not changed after.

TestResult
E3 known case (GEBN)Passed for the four. 4 of 4 publicly confirmed GEBN leaders found as Tier A with DOIs. A fifth name could not be confirmed from a public source.
E1 placebo lift (Tier B)FAIL. Lift 0.96x (interval 0.84-1.09) against works-count-matched random groups: Tier B is not a sign of unusual closeness.
E2 temporal hold-outFAIL. The weighted score (AUC 0.716) does not beat publication volume (0.720) or a plain count of Tier A co-authors (0.749).
E6 mega-paper ablationWithout the 25-author cap A1 would grow from 949 to 1,069 people and Tier B from 1,309 to 1,889; 23.7% of A1+B people exist only through big papers. The cap is kept.
E8 golden tests15 of 15 pass.
E9 concurrencyTwo server processes checking and ruling at the same time: nothing lost.
E10 tenant isolationAll 13 tools tried with another newsroom's ids: nothing visible.

Because E1 and E2 failed, only Tier A is a finding. Tier B, Tier C and the score are shown as labelled network context, and experts are never ranked by score.

6Who decides

The model never records a verdict and never approves a sourcing note. On this page those are buttons: you click, read what will happen (for a note, the whole note), and confirm a second time. Excluding a paper re-runs every saved check in the newsroom and shows what changed; merging two split profiles is possible through the API only. A sourcing note can be approved only after the Verifier has checked every claim against the graph.

7Limits

  • Funding data is thin. Many papers list no funder, so "no tie found" often means "no funding recorded".
  • Split author profiles. OpenAlex sometimes splits one person into several records; a check on the wrong record can miss evidence. A human can merge two profiles.
  • The graph is a sample. Co-authors' other works are not loaded, so ties through unloaded papers are invisible.
  • Gifts to universities and other money that OpenAlex funder fields do not record are invisible.
  • Profile merge and split error rate is unknown. The hand check (E5) was cut.

8For judges

  • The MCP server has 13 tools and makes no LLM calls. Claude Code is the agent and the MCP client; this page only shows its steps.
  • Every model-reachable read uses GRAPH.RO_QUERY with a timeout and a row cap; writes are fixed, parameterised queries. Every tool returns the Cypher it ran, and the live evidence panel shows it.
  • In the web app the model gets the read tools and the memory-writing tools (open_case, check_case, recheck, draft_note, which save cases, checks and draft notes), but never record_verdict or approve_note: those run only from a confirmed click on the page.
